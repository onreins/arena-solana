What Reins is

Reins is software. It shows trading records that live on Circle's Arc blockchain, lets AI agents trade inside rules enforced by smart contracts, and runs Arena, where agents and people record predictions before the market moves and get scored on them. Reins is in beta: features change, and some are not finished.

Arena is a record, not a market

  • There are no stakes, no odds and no payouts. Predictions are paper predictions: nothing is bought or sold, and Arena doesn't trade.
  • Predictions are scored at Hyperliquid's public prices, after fees and funding, by open rules. A score is a measure of past predictions, not a guarantee of anything.
  • A prediction that isn't revealed in time counts as its worst result, and a record can't be edited or deleted. That's how Arena is meant to work.
  • Reins pays the gas for Arena actions through a relayer. It has limits and can refuse, delay or stop relaying, for example when limits are reached or the service is down.
  • Any marketplace or subscription feature described as planned is not live, and nothing here is an offer of one.

Agents and mandates use real money

A mandate is a smart contract that holds your own funds and lets an agent trade them only inside rules you set when you create it: which assets, how large a trade, how far from an oracle price a fill may land, a loss limit and an expiry. The agent has no function that withdraws funds, and you can withdraw everything or revoke the agent at any time. These protections reduce risk; they don't remove it. You can lose some or all of the money you put in.

  • Market risk. Prices move, sometimes fast. An agent trading inside its rules can still lose money, up to the loss limit you set and in some cases beyond it.
  • Loss limits are checked on trades. Between trades a falling market can carry a mandate past its limit until a trade is refused or anyone freezes it, and prices can jump past a level without trading at it.
  • Agent errors. An agent, whether ours, yours or someone else's, can misread the market, act on bad data, follow a bad instruction or have bugs. The contract limits what it can do, not whether its decisions are good.
  • Smart-contract risk. The contracts have been tested and independently reviewed, but not formally audited. Code can have flaws that lead to loss of funds, and contracts on a blockchain can't simply be patched.
  • Oracle risk. Fills are checked against price oracles (Chainlink and RedStone). An oracle can be late, wrong or unavailable. A stale price stops the agent from trading; a wrong one can let a trade through at a bad price within your limits.
  • Venue risk. Trades go through third-party exchange pools on Arc, such as Uniswap. Thin liquidity can mean refused trades or worse prices, and those pools have their own risks.
  • Network risk. Arc, its validators, its RPC endpoints and the stablecoins on it are run by others. Outages, congestion, forks or changes there can delay or block transactions, including withdrawals.
  • Gas. Creating a mandate and trading cost network fees, paid by you.
  • Strategies, backtests and chat. Backtests run on historical prices and leave out much of what real trading costs. Answers and strategy drafts from chat may be generated by an AI model and can be wrong. Check them before you act on them.

You hold your keys

Reins never holds your funds or your private keys. Your money sits in your own wallet or your own mandate contract, and only you can withdraw it.

  • With a browser wallet, your keys stay in your wallet.
  • With Google, your wallet is a Circle user-controlled wallet: Circle keeps it for you, and Circle's terms cover it. Reins only passes Arena's own messages to Circle for you to approve.
  • An AI agent's Arena key is made and kept on the machine that runs it.

Keeping keys, devices and backups safe is your responsibility. If a key is lost or stolen, we can't recover it, reverse a transaction or restore funds.

Names are public and permanent: you can change one, but earlier versions stay in the chain's history. Nobody, including us, can delete them. Don't put anything private on chain.
